1. Geography & Environment

  • Strait of Hormuz: Links the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman (leading into the Arabian Sea). World's premier oil transit corridor.
  • Chumbi Valley Passes: Nathu La and Jelep La (Sikkim) connect India with the strategic Chumbi Valley in Tibet.
  • San Andreas Fault: Prominent continental transform plate boundary between the Pacific Plate and the North American Plate in California.
  • Land of Red River & Blue Hills: Traditional sobriquet for Assam (Red River = Brahmaputra; Blue Hills = surrounding peaks).
  • Mouling National Park: Situated in Arunachal Pradesh (Upper Siang district).
  • Arabian Sea Coastline: Gujarat possesses the longest coastline in India as well as along the Arabian Sea.
  • Triveni Canal: Originates from the Gandak River in West Champaran, Bihar.
  • Tilaiya Dam: Built across the Barakar River under the Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC).
  • Atomic Minerals: Uranium, Thorium, Monazite, and Zircon are nuclear/atomic minerals; Lead is an ordinary base metal.
  • Ramsar Convention: Signed on February 2, 1971, in Ramsar, Iran for wetland preservation.
  • Minamata Disease: Neurological condition caused by bioaccumulation of toxic Methylmercury.
  • Renuka Lake: Largest natural freshwater lake in Himachal Pradesh (Sirmaur district); Ramsar site.
  • Rhine River: Heaviest used and busiest inland industrial waterway across Western Europe.
  • Mount McKinley (Denali): Highest peak in North America (6,190 m), located in Alaska, USA.
  • Normal Lapse Rate: Tropospheric temperature decreases at the standard rate of 6.5 °C per 1,000 meters (1 km).
  • Nor'westers (Kalbaisakhi): Local convective pre-monsoon thunder-showers in West Bengal & Assam.
  • Marina Beach: Located in Chennai; India's longest natural urban beach.
  • UNESCO Sites in Bihar: Mahabodhi Temple Complex (Bodh Gaya, 2002) and Nalanda Mahavihara (2016).
  • Bihar-UP Border (North to South): West Champaran → Gopalganj → Siwan → Saran → Bhojpur → Buxar → Kaimur → Rohtas.
  • First OTEC Plant: Developed by NIOT at Kavaratti, Lakshadweep for ocean thermal desalination.
  • Zoji La Pass: Connects Srinagar with Leh across the Great Himalayan range.
  • Devprayag Confluence: Confluence of Alaknanda + Bhagirathi rivers, forming the official Ganga.
  • Karewas: Lacustrine clay/silt terrace formations in the Kashmir Valley, ideal for saffron (zafran) farming.
  • Mount Kilimanjaro: Dormant volcanic peak in Tanzania; highest mountain summit in Africa (5,895 m).
  • Coal Reserves: Jharkhand holds the highest recoverable coal reserves in India.
  • Bihar Census 2011: Population density is 1,106 per sq km (highest in India); Sex ratio is 918.
  • Bihar Economic Survey: First published for the financial year 2006–07.

2. History & National Movement

  • Veer Kunwar Singh (1857):** The 80-year-old Zamindar of Jagdishpur led the revolt, joining mutineers at **Arrah** in July 1857.
  • Deccan Riots (1875): Immediate trigger was extortion and exorbitant debt-interest bonds by immigrant moneylenders (Mahajans/Sahukars).
  • Satyashodhak Samaj: Founded in 1873 by Jyotirao Phule in Maharashtra for the upliftment of oppressed classes.
  • Central Assembly Bombing (1929): Bhagat Singh threw bombs on April 8, 1929, when he was a little more than 21 years old.
  • Rowlatt Satyagraha: Protest against the Rowlatt Act & arrests of Dr. Kitchlew/Dr. Satyapal led to the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (April 13, 1919).
  • R.P. Dutt's Thesis: In *India Today*, Rajani Palme Dutt criticized INC as having a dual character—fighting yet collaborating with imperialism.
  • Santhal Hool (1855–56): Massive tribal insurrection against British revenue agents and Mahajans led by Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu.
  • 1857 Critique: R.C. Majumdar concluded that 1857 was "Neither first, nor national, nor a war of independence".
  • 1st INC Session (1885): Held in Bombay (Gokuldas Tejpal College), attended by 72 delegates; presided by W.C. Bonnerjee.
  • Servants of India Society: Established in 1905 by Gopal Krishna Gokhale in Pune.
  • Champaran Satyagraha (1917): Gandhi's first civil disobedience in India against the oppressive Tinkathia system (3/20 indigo).
  • Lahore Session (1929): Presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru; adopted the goal of Purna Swaraj.
  • The Bengalee: Influential nationalist newspaper founded/edited by Surendranath Banerjee.
  • Swaraj Party (1923): Formed by C.R. Das (President) and Motilal Nehru (Secretary) to obstruct councils from within.
  • Belgaum Session (1924): The only INC annual session presided over by Mahatma Gandhi.
  • Patna Salt Satyagraha (1930): Led by Ambika Kant Sinha at Nakhas Pond (Nakhas Pind).

3. Indian Polity & Constitution

  • Article 17: Abolition of 'Untouchability' and penal prohibition of its practice.
  • Article 32: Fundamental Right to Constitutional Remedies; described as the "Heart and Soul of the Constitution" by Dr. Ambedkar.
  • Article 213 vs 123 (Ordinances): Article 213 empowers the Governor; Article 123 empowers the President.
  • Article 280: Mandates the appointment of a Finance Commission every 5 years by the President.
  • Article 352: Proclamation of National Emergency on grounds of War, External Aggression, or Armed Rebellion.
  • Writ of Prohibition: Issued by higher judiciary to inferior courts/tribunals to stop them from exceeding jurisdiction.
  • Presidential Eligibility: Minimum qualifying age is 35 years (same for Vice-President & Governor).
  • Rajya Sabha Maximum Strength: Capped at 250 members (238 elected + 12 nominated by President).
  • Joint Sitting of Parliament: Summoned under Article 108; presided over by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
  • Directive Principles (DPSP): Borrowed from the Irish Constitution (Part IV).
  • Local Self-Government: 1st Panchayati Raj launched on Oct 2, 1959, in Nagaur, Rajasthan. 73rd & 74th Amendments gave constitutional status to Panchayats & Municipalities.

4. General Science

  • Bauxite: Primary natural ore from which Aluminium (Al) is extracted.
  • Baking Soda: Chemically known as Sodium Bicarbonate (NaHCO₃).
  • Oil of Vitriol: Historical term for concentrated Sulphuric Acid (H₂SO₄).
  • pH of Pure Water: Neutral value of exactly 7 at 25 °C.
  • Gravity (g): Maximum at Earth's Poles (~9.83 m/s²) and minimum at the Equator (~9.78 m/s²).
  • Total Internal Reflection (TIR): The foundational physical principle behind Optical Fibers and mirages.
  • Current & Resistance: Current is measured with an Ammeter in series; Resistance unit is Ohm (Ω).
  • Brass Alloy: Made of Copper (Cu) + Zinc (Zn).
  • Stomach Acid: Gastric glands secrete Hydrochloric Acid (HCl) to activate pepsin.
  • Vitamin D: Synthesized photochemically in skin cells upon exposure to solar UV-B radiation.
  • Mitochondria: Known as the "Powerhouse of the Cell"; primary site of cellular ATP production.
  • RBC Formation (Erythropoiesis): Formed in healthy adults within the Red Bone Marrow.
  • Universal Donor: O negative (O⁻) blood group (lacks A, B, and Rh surface antigens).
  • Insulin Hormone: Synthesized by the β-cells of the Islets of Langerhans in the Pancreas.
  • Ocean Acidification: Driven by dissolution of atmospheric Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) into oceanic waters.

5. Economic & Rural Models

  • PURA Model: Providing Urban Amenities to Rural Areas was strongly championed by former President Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam.
  • Microfinance & SHGs: Pioneer of modern microcredit and Grameen model is Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus.
