## GS-I and GS-II: practice questions with answer frameworks

Practice questions in the BPSC Mains style, written for this site (not official papers). Write each answer in about 7–8 minutes, then open the framework.

### GS-I

<details><summary><b>Q1.</b> Assess the significance of the Champaran Satyagraha (1917) in India's freedom struggle.</summary>

- Background: tinkathia system, indigo planters' oppression; Rajkumar Shukla's invitation.
- Events: Gandhi defies order to leave; inquiry with Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad, J.B. Kripalani; Champaran Agrarian Act 1918.
- Significance: first satyagraha in India; brought peasants into the national movement; created Bihar's leadership; method of non-violent mass inquiry.
- Conclusion: laboratory of Gandhian techniques later used in Kheda and Non-Cooperation.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Q2.</b> Describe Bihar's contribution to the Quit India Movement.</summary>

- 11 Aug 1942: seven students martyred at Patna Secretariat; railway and telegraph disruption; parallel governments.
- JP's escape from Hazaribagh jail (Nov 1942) and Azad Dasta.
- Role of students, peasants and women; repression and collective fines.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Q3.</b> Nalanda was more than a university. Discuss.</summary>

- Founded by Kumaragupta I; curriculum (Buddhist philosophy, logic, grammar, medicine); foreign scholars (Xuanzang, Yijing); library (Dharmaganja).
- A centre of diplomacy and Asian cultural exchange; Pala patronage.
- Decline and revival: UNESCO 2016; new Nalanda University (2010 Act, campus 2024).
</details>

<details><summary><b>Q4.</b> Discuss the role of folk art in the economy and identity of Bihar.</summary>

- Madhubani (GI 2007), Manjusha, Tikuli, Sikki grass craft; women artists and Padma awards.
- Economic: livelihoods, exports, tourism, e-commerce, GI premium.
- Challenges: middlemen, copying, low wages. Way forward: Upendra Maharathi Shilp Anusandhan Sansthan training, market linkages, ODOP.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Q5 (statistics).</b> The table shows rice production (lakh tonnes) in a district: 2021: 4.0, 2022: 4.4, 2023: 4.2, 2024: 4.8. Find the average, the year-on-year changes and the overall percentage growth.</summary>

- Average = 17.4/4 = **4.35** lakh tonnes.
- Changes: +10% (2022), −4.55% (2023), +14.29% (2024).
- Overall growth 2021→2024 = (4.8 − 4.0)/4.0 = **20%**.
- Comment: rising trend with a dip in 2023 (perhaps rainfall).
</details>

### GS-II

<details><summary><b>Q6.</b> How far has the 50% reservation for women in panchayats changed governance in Bihar?</summary>

- Bihar Panchayat Raj Act 2006: first state; later many states followed.
- Gains: women's visibility, focus on water, schools, health; leadership pipeline.
- Problems: "mukhiya pati" (proxy), low literacy, limited funds and powers.
- Way forward: training, devolution of funds, functions and functionaries (3Fs), SHG convergence.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Q7.</b> Floods in north Bihar are an annual disaster. Explain the causes and suggest a long-term strategy.</summary>

- Causes: Himalayan rivers (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati) with high silt, heavy monsoon rain in Nepal, flat terrain, embankment failures (Kusaha 2008), encroachment of floodplains.
- Effects: loss of life, crops, migration, health.
- Strategy: India–Nepal cooperation (Kosi high dam proposal), flood forecasting, river interlinking (Kosi–Mechi link), wetland restoration, flood-resilient housing and crops, SDRF.
- Map: draw north Bihar rivers.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Q8.</b> Examine the role of digital governance in public service delivery in Bihar.</summary>

- RTPS Act 2011, Lok Shikayat Act 2015, e-Kalyan, DBT, land-record digitisation, Jeevika digital finance.
- Benefits: transparency, time-bound services, less corruption.
- Limits: digital divide, connectivity, literacy. Way forward: common service centres, Hindi-language apps, training.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Q9.</b> Explain the significance of the 73rd Amendment and the challenges of local self-government.</summary>

- Three tiers, regular elections, State Election Commission, State Finance Commission, reservation, Gram Sabha, 11th Schedule (29 subjects).
- Challenges: weak finances, state control, capacity, parallel bodies.
- Bihar example: Gram Kachahari (village courts) under the 2006 Act.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Q10.</b> How can science and technology help Bihar's agriculture?</summary>

- Seeds and biotech (BAU Sabour), soil health cards, weather advisories, drones, solar pumps, cold chains, e-NAM, makhana mechanisation (ICAR–NRC Makhana, Darbhanga).
- Conclusion: technology plus institutions (FPOs) can double farm incomes.
</details>
