> **Unit 1 · Micro Economics.** Usually 10–15% of the subject paper. Questions test definitions, diagrams, conditions (MR = MC, MRS = price ratio) and who said what.

## 1. Theory of demand

### Cardinal utility (Marshall)
- Utility is measurable in "utils". **Law of diminishing marginal utility** (Gossen's first law).
- Equilibrium with one good: MUx = Px (marginal utility of money constant).
- With many goods, **equi-marginal principle** (Gossen's second law): MUx/Px = MUy/Py = … = MUm.
- **Consumer surplus** = what you are willing to pay − what you pay (area under demand above price). Marshall; Hicks gave the compensating and equivalent variation versions.

### Ordinal utility (Hicks–Allen, 1934)
- Indifference curves (IC): downward sloping, convex to origin (diminishing **MRS**), never intersect, higher IC = higher satisfaction.
- **MRSxy = MUx / MUy** = −dy/dx along an IC.
- Budget line: Px·x + Py·y = M; slope = −Px/Py.
- **Equilibrium: MRSxy = Px/Py**, and the IC must be convex at that point (second-order condition).

### Price, income and substitution effects
- **Price effect = Substitution effect + Income effect.**
- **Hicks** substitution effect keeps *utility* constant (compensating variation). **Slutsky** keeps *purchasing power* constant (the old bundle stays just affordable).
- Substitution effect is **always negative** (price ↑ → quantity ↓).
- Normal good: income effect reinforces → demand slopes down.
- Inferior good: income effect works against, but substitution effect is stronger → demand still slopes down.
- **Giffen good**: inferior AND income effect > substitution effect → demand slopes **up**. All Giffen goods are inferior; not all inferior goods are Giffen.
- **Slutsky equation**: ∂x/∂p = ∂h/∂p − x·∂x/∂M (h = Hicksian/compensated demand).

### Revealed preference (Samuelson, 1938)
- Based on observed choices, no ICs needed. **Weak axiom (WARP)**: if A is revealed preferred to B, B can never be revealed preferred to A. **Strong axiom (SARP)** adds transitivity (Houthakker).

### Elasticity
| Type | Formula | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Price elasticity (ed) | (%ΔQ)/(%ΔP) | ed > 1 elastic; on a straight-line demand curve ed = lower segment / upper segment |
| Income elasticity | %ΔQ / %ΔM | < 0 inferior, 0–1 necessity, > 1 luxury |
| Cross elasticity | %ΔQx / %ΔPy | > 0 substitutes, < 0 complements |
| Arc elasticity | (ΔQ/ΔP)·(P1+P2)/(Q1+Q2) | used for large changes |

- **MR = P(1 − 1/ed)**. So a monopolist never produces where ed < 1 (MR would be negative).
- Total revenue is maximum where ed = 1.

### Other demand ideas
- **Veblen effect** (conspicuous consumption), **bandwagon** and **snob** effects (Leibenstein).
- **Lancaster**: consumers demand *characteristics* of goods.
- **Engel curve**: quantity vs income. **Engel's law**: share of income spent on food falls as income rises.
- Expected utility (von Neumann–Morgenstern): risk averse = concave utility; risk premium; certainty equivalent < expected value for risk-averse people. **St. Petersburg paradox** → Bernoulli.

## 2. Production and costs

### Production function
- Short run: **law of variable proportions** (three stages; rational producer operates in **Stage II**, where MP > 0 and AP is falling).
- Long run: **returns to scale**: increasing, constant, decreasing.
- **Isoquant** slope = **MRTS(L,K) = MPL / MPK**. Producer equilibrium: MRTS = w/r.
- **Cobb–Douglas**: Q = A·L^α·K^β.
  - α + β = 1 → constant returns; α, β are output elasticities and (under perfect competition) factor shares.
  - Elasticity of substitution σ = 1.
- **CES** (Arrow, Chenery, Minhas, Solow, 1961): σ constant but not necessarily 1. **Leontief** (fixed proportions): σ = 0.
- **Euler's theorem**: with constant returns, paying each factor its MP exhausts output (product exhaustion).
- Expansion path, ridge lines (boundaries of the economic region of production).

### Costs
- TC = TFC + TVC. AC = AFC + AVC. **MC = dTC/dQ**.
- MC cuts **AVC and AC at their minimum points**. Minimum AVC = **shutdown point**; minimum AC = **break-even point**.
- Long-run AC is the **envelope** of short-run AC curves (Viner's draftsman mistake: LAC is not tangent at SAC minimum except at the LAC minimum).
- U-shape of LAC: economies then diseconomies of scale. Modern view: **L-shaped** LAC.

## 3. Market structures

| Market | Firms | Product | Equilibrium | Long-run profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect competition | many | homogeneous | P = MR = MC | normal (P = min LAC) |
| Monopoly | one | no close substitute | MR = MC < P | can be supernormal |
| Monopolistic competition (Chamberlin, 1933) | many | differentiated | MR = MC; tangency P = LAC | normal, with **excess capacity** |
| Oligopoly | few | either | interdependent | varies |

### Perfect competition
- Firm supply curve = MC curve above min AVC. Allocative efficiency (P = MC) and productive efficiency (min LAC).

### Monopoly
- Equilibrium: MR = MC and MC cutting MR from below. Price > MC → **deadweight loss**.
- **Lerner index** = (P − MC)/P = 1/ed.
- **Price discrimination** (Pigou): 1st degree (perfect, takes all consumer surplus), 2nd degree (by blocks of quantity), 3rd degree (by market: charge more where demand is less elastic; MR1 = MR2 = MC).
- **Bilateral monopoly**: one seller, one buyer; price is indeterminate within a range.

### Oligopoly models
| Model | Decision variable | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| **Cournot** (1838) | quantity, simultaneous | each of 2 firms produces 1/3 of competitive output; together 2/3 |
| **Bertrand** (1883) | price, simultaneous | with identical goods and costs, P = MC (Bertrand paradox) |
| **Stackelberg** (1934) | quantity, leader–follower | leader produces more and earns more |
| **Sweezy kinked demand** (1939) | price | explains **price rigidity**; discontinuous MR |
| **Edgeworth** | price with capacity limits | price keeps oscillating |
| Cartel / collusion | joint profit | unstable (each firm wants to cheat) |
| **Baumol** | sales revenue maximisation subject to minimum profit | output higher than profit-maximiser |
| **Marris** | growth maximisation | managerial theory |
| **Williamson** | managerial utility (staff, perks) | managerial discretion |
| **Bain / Sylos-Labini** | limit pricing | price set low to deter entry |

## 4. Game theory
- **Nash equilibrium**: no player can gain by changing strategy alone.
- **Dominant strategy**: best whatever the other does.
- **Prisoner's dilemma**: both confess (dominant), though both would gain by staying silent. Explains why cartels break down.
- Zero-sum games: **minimax = maximin** gives the saddle point (von Neumann & Morgenstern, *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*, 1944).
- Repeated games: cooperation possible (tit-for-tat). Subgame perfect equilibrium: backward induction (Selten).

## 5. Factor pricing and distribution
- **Marginal productivity theory**: factor paid VMP (perfect competition) = MRP. Under monopoly in product market, factor gets MRP < VMP (**monopolistic exploitation**, Joan Robinson). Monopsony in factor market → **monopsonistic exploitation**.
- **Rent**: Ricardian (differential fertility; "rent is not a cost, it is price-determined"), **quasi-rent** (Marshall; short-run earnings of fixed factors above variable cost), **transfer earnings** (minimum to keep a factor in its present use; economic rent = earnings − transfer earnings, the modern view of Joan Robinson and others).
- **Interest**: classical (saving–investment), **loanable funds** (Robertson, Ohlin), **liquidity preference** (Keynes), time preference (Fisher, Böhm-Bawerk agio).
- **Profit**: risk (Hawley), uncertainty (**Knight**: insurable risk vs uninsurable uncertainty), innovation (**Schumpeter**), dynamic changes (J.B. Clark).
- Wage: backward-bending labour supply (income effect beats substitution effect at high wages).
- **Kaldor's** distribution theory: shares depend on saving propensities of capitalists and workers.

## 6. General equilibrium and welfare
- **Walras**: equilibrium in all markets at once; **Walras's law**: if n−1 markets clear, the nth clears too.
- **Edgeworth box**: **contract curve** = set of Pareto-efficient allocations.
- **Pareto optimality** conditions: (1) MRS equal for all consumers (exchange), (2) MRTS equal for all producers (production), (3) MRS = MRT (product mix).
- **First fundamental theorem**: competitive equilibrium is Pareto optimal. **Second**: any Pareto optimum can be reached by competition after a suitable lump-sum redistribution.
- **Compensation criteria**: Kaldor (gainers *could* compensate losers), Hicks (losers could not bribe gainers to stop the change), **Scitovsky** double criterion (removes reversal paradox), **Little** criterion (adds distribution).
- **Social welfare function**: Bergson (1938), Samuelson. **Arrow's impossibility theorem** (1951): no social choice rule satisfies unrestricted domain, Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives and non-dictatorship together.
- **Amartya Sen**: capability approach; liberal paradox ("impossibility of a Paretian liberal").
- **Theory of second best** (Lipsey & Lancaster, 1956): if one Pareto condition cannot be met, meeting the others may not improve welfare.

## 7. Economics of information
- **Adverse selection**: hidden information before a deal (Akerlof's *Market for Lemons*, 1970).
- **Moral hazard**: hidden action after a deal (insurance makes people careless).
- **Signalling** (Spence: education as a signal) and **screening** (Stiglitz). Nobel 2001: Akerlof, Spence, Stiglitz.
- **Principal–agent problem**: incentive contracts.

## Exam traps
- Substitution effect is negative for **all** goods, including Giffen goods.
- Monopolist operates only on the **elastic** part of the demand curve.
- Under perfect competition the firm's demand curve is **horizontal** (perfectly elastic) but the industry demand curve slopes down.
- In the long run under monopolistic competition, profit is normal but output is below the minimum-LAC level (excess capacity).
- Cobb–Douglas always has σ = 1, whatever the returns to scale.
