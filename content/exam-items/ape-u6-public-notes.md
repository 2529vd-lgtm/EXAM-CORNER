> **Unit 6 · Public Economics.** Mixes theory (public goods, incidence, Musgrave) with Indian facts (Finance Commission, GST, FRBM). Both come every year.

## 1. Role of government (Musgrave)
Richard Musgrave, *The Theory of Public Finance* (1959): three branches of government budget:
1. **Allocation**: provide public goods, correct market failure.
2. **Distribution**: fair distribution of income and wealth.
3. **Stabilisation**: full employment and price stability.

**Market failure** sources: public goods, externalities, natural monopoly, information asymmetry, incomplete markets.

## 2. Public goods
- **Pure public good**: **non-rival** and **non-excludable** (defence, street light). Leads to the **free-rider problem**.
- **Samuelson condition** (1954): efficient supply where **ΣMRS = MRT** (vertical summation of individual demand curves). For private goods we sum horizontally.
- **Lindahl equilibrium**: each person pays a tax-price equal to their marginal benefit (voluntary exchange; Wicksell–Lindahl).
- **Merit goods** (Musgrave): health, education, provided more than people would buy. **Demerit goods**: tobacco, alcohol.
- **Club goods**: excludable but non-rival (Buchanan, 1965). **Common pool resources**: rival but non-excludable → **tragedy of the commons** (Hardin, 1968).
- **Tiebout model** (1956): people "vote with their feet" among local governments.

## 3. Externalities
- Negative externality → overproduction. **Pigouvian tax** = marginal external cost at the efficient output.
- Positive externality → underproduction → subsidy.
- **Coase theorem** (1960): with clear property rights and zero transaction costs, bargaining gives an efficient outcome regardless of who holds the rights.

## 4. Public choice
- **Median voter theorem** (Black, Downs): majority voting picks the median voter's preferred option.
- **Condorcet / voting paradox**: majority voting can be intransitive.
- **Arrow's impossibility theorem**.
- **Buchanan & Tullock**, *The Calculus of Consent* (1962); Buchanan Nobel 1986. Rent-seeking (Tullock, Krueger). Bureaucrats maximise budgets (**Niskanen**).

## 5. Public expenditure theories
| Theory | Author | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Law of increasing state activity** | Adolph **Wagner** (1883) | public spending grows faster than national income as economies develop |
| **Displacement effect** | **Peacock & Wiseman** (1961) | spending rises in steps during crises (wars) and does not return to the old level; tolerance level of taxation |
| Critical limit hypothesis | Colin Clark | taxes above ~25% of national income cause inflation |
| Principle of maximum social advantage | **Dalton** (Pigou also) | spend and tax until marginal social benefit = marginal social sacrifice |
| Pure theory | Musgrave, Samuelson | as above |

## 6. Taxation
### Canons of taxation (Adam Smith)
Equality, certainty, convenience, economy. Later additions: productivity, elasticity, flexibility, simplicity, diversity.

### Principles of equity
- **Benefit principle** (Lindahl, Wicksell).
- **Ability-to-pay**: horizontal equity (equals treated equally), vertical equity (unequals unequally). Sacrifice theories: **equal absolute**, **equal proportional**, **equal marginal (least aggregate) sacrifice** (Edgeworth, Pigou): the last gives the most progressive tax.

### Tax structure
- **Progressive** (average rate rises with income), **proportional**, **regressive**.
- **Direct** (incidence and impact on same person: income tax) vs **indirect** (shifted: GST).

### Incidence and shifting
- **Impact** = who pays first; **incidence** = who finally bears the burden; **effect** = economic consequences.
- **Rule**: the tax burden falls more on the side of the market that is **less elastic**. Buyer's share = es/(es + ed); seller's share = ed/(es + ed).
- Perfectly inelastic demand → buyers bear all; perfectly elastic demand → sellers bear all.
- Deadweight loss of a tax ≈ ½·t²·(elasticity term); rises with the **square** of the tax rate (Harberger).
- **Ramsey rule** (optimal commodity tax): tax goods in inverse proportion to their demand elasticity (inverse elasticity rule).
- **Optimal income tax**: Mirrlees (1971).
- **Laffer curve**: beyond some rate, higher tax rates reduce revenue.

## 7. Budget and deficits (India)
| Deficit | Definition |
|---|---|
| Revenue deficit | revenue expenditure − revenue receipts |
| Effective revenue deficit | revenue deficit − grants for creation of capital assets |
| **Fiscal deficit** | total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts) = **total borrowing** |
| Primary deficit | fiscal deficit − interest payments |
| Monetised deficit | RBI's net credit to government |

- **FRBM Act 2003**; N.K. Singh Committee (2017) recommended **debt as the anchor** (general government debt 60% of GDP: Centre 40%, States 20%) with fiscal deficit as the operating target.
- Union Budget 2026-27: fiscal deficit target **4.3% of GDP**, debt-to-GDP about **55.6%**, aim to bring debt to about 50% by 2030 (see "Current Economy 2026").
- Types of budget: zero-based (Peter Pyhrr), performance budgeting, outcome budget (India 2005), gender budgeting (India 2005-06), green budget.

## 8. Public debt
- **Ricardian equivalence** (Barro, 1974): debt and taxes have the same effect, since people save for future taxes.
- Burden of debt: internal debt "we owe it to ourselves" (Lerner); Buchanan argued burden shifts to future generations. **Domar's condition**: debt/GDP stabilises if growth rate > interest rate.
- Debt redemption: sinking fund, conversion, refunding, capital levy.

## 9. Fiscal federalism
- **Oates' decentralisation theorem**: local public goods are better provided by local governments.
- **Vertical imbalance**: Centre has more revenue powers; states have more spending duties. **Horizontal imbalance**: between states.
- **Finance Commission** (Article **280**), every five years.

| FC | Chair | Period | States' share in divisible pool |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14th | Y.V. Reddy | 2015–20 | **42%** |
| 15th | N.K. Singh | 2020–26 | **41%** (after J&K became a UT) |
| 16th | Arvind Panagariya | 2026–31 | **41%** retained (accepted in Budget 2026-27) |

- Grants-in-aid under **Article 275**. Article 282: discretionary grants. Article 270: sharing of central taxes.

## 10. GST
- Launched **1 July 2017**; **101st Constitutional Amendment** (2016). **GST Council: Article 279A**, chaired by the Union Finance Minister; states have 2/3 of voting weight, Centre 1/3; decisions need 3/4 majority.
- Destination-based consumption tax; CGST + SGST on intra-state supply, **IGST** on inter-state supply.
- Compensation to states for 5 years (till June 2022), cess extended for loan repayment.
- **GST 2.0 (Sept 2025)**: rates rationalised mainly into two slabs, **5% and 18%**, with a **40%** rate for sin/luxury goods. (Check the latest Council notifications.)
- Taxes outside GST: alcohol for human consumption, petroleum crude, petrol, diesel, ATF, natural gas (to be brought in on Council's recommendation), stamp duty, electricity.

## Exam traps
- Pure public good efficiency: ΣMRS = MRT, demand curves summed **vertically**.
- Fiscal deficit equals government borrowing, not the revenue gap.
- Tax burden falls on the **less elastic** side.
- Wagner's law is about spending growing faster than income, not about taxes.
- Peacock–Wiseman: the displacement effect happens in "jumps" after social disturbances.
