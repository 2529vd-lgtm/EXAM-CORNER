## 2. Structure of the economy
- **Services** have the largest share of GSDP (trade, hotels, transport, real estate, public administration). Agriculture's share in GSDP is around a fifth, but it employs about half of the workforce: low productivity.
- **Industry** share is small; manufacturing is weak. Main units: food processing, sugar, dairy (COMFED "Sudha"), leather, textiles, cement, petroleum refinery (Barauni).
- Large **remittance** economy: heavy out-migration of workers to Delhi, Punjab, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Gulf.

## 3. Agriculture
- Fertile Gangetic plains; three seasons (kharif, rabi, zaid). Main crops: **rice, wheat, maize** (Bihar is a leading maize producer), pulses, sugarcane, jute (Purnia–Katihar), litchi, makhana, vegetables.
- **Makhana**: Bihar produces about **85–90%** of India's makhana; **Mithila Makhana got a GI tag (2022)**; **Makhana Board** announced in Union Budget 2025-26.
- **Litchi**: Muzaffarpur's **Shahi Litchi** (GI 2018). Other GIs: Bhagalpur **Jardalu** mango, **Katarni** rice, **Magahi paan**, Silao **khaja**, Bhagalpur silk, Madhubani painting.
- **Agriculture Road Maps**: 1st (2008–12), 2nd (2012–17), 3rd (2017–22, extended), **4th (2023–28)**.
- Problems: small holdings (over 90% marginal farmers), floods in the north, drought in the south, low irrigation in south Bihar, low mechanisation, weak markets (APMC Act repealed in **2006**, so no regulated mandis).

## 4. Industry and infrastructure
- **Bihar Industrial Investment Promotion Policy** 2016 (and later updates); Bihar Textile & Leather Policy 2022; Bihar Logistics Policy 2023; Global Investors' Summit "Bihar Business Connect" (2023, 2024).
- Power: Barh, Kahalgaon, Nabinagar (NTPC). Bihar has near-universal household electrification.
- Roads and bridges: Ganga bridges (Mahatma Gandhi Setu, Kacchi Dargah–Bidupur), expressways; Patna Metro (first section opened Oct 2025).
- Industrial corridor: Amritsar–Kolkata Industrial Corridor passes through Bihar (Gaya node).

## 5. Poverty, unemployment and migration
- Highest share of multidimensionally poor (NITI MPI). Caste survey: about **34% of families earn up to ₹6,000 a month**.
- Unemployment is mostly **disguised** and seasonal; low female labour participation (improving through SHGs).
- **Migration**: push (floods, small farms, few factories) and pull (wages elsewhere). Remittances support rural consumption.

## 6. Social sector
- **Jeevika** (Bihar Rural Livelihoods Promotion Society, 2006, World Bank support): over 1 crore women in SHGs; model later used in NRLM.
- Education: bicycle scheme for girls (2006), Mukhyamantri Kanya Utthan Yojana, teacher recruitment through BPSC TRE (2023–24).
- Health: improvement in IMR and institutional delivery; doctor and hospital shortages remain.
- **Liquor prohibition** since **5 April 2016**: social gains claimed; revenue loss and enforcement problems debated.
- **Mukhyamantri Mahila Rozgar Yojana** (2025): ₹10,000 first instalment to a woman in each family to start self-employment.

## 8. State finances
- Low **own tax revenue** (about ₹65,800 crore expected in 2026-27); heavy dependence on **central tax share** (Bihar got the second-largest share after UP under the 15th Finance Commission, about 10%; check the 16th FC's state-wise shares) and grants.
- Fiscal deficit target 3% of GSDP; committed expenditure is 47% of revenue receipts.
- Special category status: demanded for years; not granted (the 14th FC ended the general/special distinction for devolution).

## 9. Why Bihar is behind: causes (use in answers)
1. **Historical**: Permanent Settlement (1793) and zamindari; freight equalisation policy (1952) took away mineral advantage; partition in 2000 took mines and industries to Jharkhand.
2. **Geographical**: floods in north (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati), drought in south.
3. **Demographic**: highest density, high fertility (TFR about 3.0 in NFHS-5).
4. **Institutional**: weak governance in the 1990s, low investment, weak land records.
5. **Structural**: low urbanisation, small industrial base.

## 10. Way forward (use in conclusions)
Agro-processing (makhana, litchi, maize, dairy), flood management with Nepal, skilling and MSMEs, urban growth centres, tourism (Buddhist and Jain circuits, Nalanda), better own-revenue collection, land-record digitisation, and women-led SHG enterprises.
