<details><summary><b>Uttar Pradesh budget 2026-27</b></summary>

- **Size:** ₹9,12,696.35 crore (about ₹9.12–9.13 lakh crore), about **12.2% more** than last year. Largest state budget in India.
- **Revenue expenditure** ₹6,64,470.55 crore; **capital expenditure** ₹2,48,225.80 crore (about 19.5% of the budget).
- **Fiscal deficit:** 3% of GSDP (about ₹1.18 lakh crore).
- **Committed expenditure** (salaries, pensions, interest): ₹3,53,743 crore, 49% of revenue receipts (PRS).
- **No new taxes.** Focus: jobs, infrastructure, health.
- New **Uttar Pradesh Employment Mission**: target of employment for 10 lakh youth.
- About ₹34,000 crore for roads and bridges (reported).
- **Supplementary budget:** ₹59,019.54 crore in the monsoon session (August 2026).
- Useful for UPESSC GK (30 questions).
</details>

<details><summary><b>Bihar budget 2026-27</b></summary>

- **Size:** ₹3,47,589.76 crore, the largest in the state's history; up by ₹30,694 crore over 2025-26.
- Framed under **"Saat Nischay-3"**.
- **GSDP 2026-27** (current prices) projected at ₹13.1 lakh crore; growth estimate 14.9% (nominal).
- **Fiscal deficit:** 3% of GSDP (₹39,112 crore). In 2025-26 (RE) it was very high, reported at 11.8% of GSDP.
- **Committed expenditure:** ₹1,33,494 crore, 47% of revenue receipts.
- **Own tax revenue** expected about ₹65,800 crore (Bihar depends heavily on central transfers).
- Rural development over ₹23,700 crore; social welfare schemes ₹7,724 crore.
- Focus: roads, housing, jobs, education, women, agriculture.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Madhya Pradesh budget 2026-27</b></summary>

- **Size:** ₹4,38,317 crore, presented by Deputy CM and Finance Minister **Jagdish Devda** on 18 Feb 2026.
- **No new taxes**; existing rates unchanged.
- **Rolling three-year budget** framework (2026-27 to 2028-29); outlay planned to reach ₹6.12 lakh crore by 2029-30.
- **GSDP** about ₹18,48,274 crore.
- **Fiscal deficit:** sources differ: 3.87–3.9% of GSDP (PRS, Drishti) vs ₹74,323 crore = 4.45% (ETV Bharat, including central special assistance; within 3% without it).
- About **₹1.27 lakh crore** for women-centred schemes; **₹23,800 crore for Ladli Behna Yojana**.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Rajasthan budget 2026-27</b></summary>

- **Size:** ₹6,10,956 crore, presented by Deputy CM and Finance Minister **Diya Kumari** on 11 Feb 2026 (her third budget), under the *Viksit Rajasthan 2047* goal.
- **Fiscal deficit:** ₹79,492 crore = **3.69% of GSDP**; overall "deficit budget" of ₹24,313 crore.
- State economy (GSDP) projected at ₹21.52 lakh crore in 2026-27.
- ₹6,800 crore for tap water; about ₹3,000 crore for solar parks in **Bikaner and Jaisalmer**; ₹25,000 crore crop loans to farmers; 400 innovative schools; jobs programme for youth.
- Assembly passed the budget with some additional reliefs.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Haryana budget 2026-27</b></summary>

- **Size:** ₹2,23,658.17 crore, presented on 2 Mar 2026 by **CM Nayab Singh Saini** (who holds Finance). Up 10.28% over 2025-26 RE (₹2,02,816.66 crore).
- **Fiscal deficit:** ₹40,293.17 crore = **2.65% of GSDP**. State debt ₹3.91 lakh crore (2026-27).
- **Capital expenditure:** ₹28,205 crore (12.6% of budget).
- New **"Haryana Agri Discom"** for farm power supply; ₹2,000/acre bonus to farmers who shift from paddy to pulses, oilseeds or cotton.
- World Bank-supported Haryana Clean Air Project (₹2,716 crore) and Water Secure Haryana Project (₹5,715 crore); ₹100 crore green climate resilience fund.
- 20% reservation for Agniveers in state police; goal of a trillion-dollar economy by 2047.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Delhi budget 2026-27</b></summary>

- **Size:** ₹1,03,700 crore, presented by **CM Rekha Gupta** on 24 Mar 2026 (her second budget). PRS puts total expenditure excluding debt repayment at ₹99,446 crore.
- Called a **"green budget"**: about 21% (≈ ₹21,600–22,200 crore) for environment and pollution control.
- Education ≈ ₹19,326 crore; health ≈ ₹13,034 crore; transport ₹8,374 crore; women and child development ₹7,406 crore (₹406 crore for free bus travel for women).
- ₹1,352 crore for dust-free roads; ₹1,000 crore to MCD for roads; free bicycles to 1.3 lakh girl students.
</details>
