## Essay paper (300 marks): how to write

In recent BPSC CCE Mains, the Essay paper has **three sections**; you write **one essay from each** (about 700–1,000 words each, 3 hours in total). Check the latest notification for the exact word limit.

### 10-minute plan before writing
1. Pick topics where you have **examples, data and Bihar links**, not the one that sounds clever.
2. Brainstorm on rough sheet: **dimensions** (social, economic, political, environmental, ethical, technological, Bihar, India, world).
3. Choose 5–6 sub-headings; order them from problem → causes → effects → efforts → way forward.

### Structure (for each essay)
| Part | What to write | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | a short story, quote, or striking fact (e.g. "Bihar's GSDP grew 13.1%, but its per-capita income is the lowest in India") and your thesis | 100–120 words |
| **Body** | 5–6 paragraphs, each with one idea, one example, one figure | 500–700 words |
| **Counter-view** | the other side, fairly | 80–100 words |
| **Way forward** | concrete steps (schemes, institutions, people) | 100 words |
| **Conclusion** | link back to the opening; hopeful, forward-looking | 60–80 words |

### Model outline: "Women's empowerment is the key to Bihar's development"
- **Opening**: Jeevika didis running a canteen in a district hospital.
- **Thesis**: when women earn and decide, families, villages and the state grow.
- **Body**: (1) political: 50% reservation in panchayats since 2006; (2) economic: over 1 crore women in Jeevika SHGs, Mukhyamantri Mahila Rozgar Yojana (2025); (3) education: bicycle scheme raised girls' enrolment; (4) social: prohibition (2016) and its link to domestic violence; (5) health: institutional deliveries; (6) remaining gaps: low female labour participation, child marriage, safety.
- **Counter-view**: proxy representation ("mukhiya pati"), dependence on cash transfers.
- **Way forward**: skilling, credit, safe transport, women in police (35% reservation in Bihar police), digital literacy.
- **Conclusion**: from "beneficiary" to "leader": Bihar's next growth engine.

### Topic bank (practise one per week)
1. Development of Bihar: from BIMARU to growth engine?
2. Migration: compulsion or opportunity?
3. Floods of north Bihar: natural disaster or man-made?
4. Education is the most powerful weapon for social change.
5. Technology and employment: friend or foe?
6. Caste census and social justice.
7. Climate change and the farmer.
8. Agriculture: the backbone of Bihar's economy.
9. Liquor prohibition: social reform or policy failure?
10. India at 100 in 2047: dreams and duties.

### Useful quotations
- "Be the change you wish to see in the world." (attributed to Gandhi)
- "Simhasan khali karo ki janta aati hai." (Dinkar; JP movement)
- "Development is freedom." (Amartya Sen)
- "Education is the most powerful weapon which you can use to change the world." (Nelson Mandela)

### Common mistakes
No sub-headings; only one dimension; no data; drifting from the topic; long introduction; no conclusion because time ran out. **Leave 10 minutes to finish every essay.**
