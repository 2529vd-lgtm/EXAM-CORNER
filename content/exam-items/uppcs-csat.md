> **CSAT (GS Paper II): maths, reasoning, comprehension, Hindi and English.** Qualifying at 33% (66/200). Many candidates fail here, so practise 30 minutes daily. Each topic: formula → worked example.

## 1. Number system
- Divisibility: by 3 (digit sum divisible by 3), by 4 (last two digits), by 8 (last three), by 9 (digit sum), by 11 (difference of alternate digit sums is 0 or divisible by 11).
- **HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers.**
- Unit digit cycles: 2 (2, 4, 8, 6), 3 (3, 9, 7, 1), 7 (7, 9, 3, 1), 8 (8, 4, 2, 6); 0, 1, 5, 6 repeat themselves.

**Example:** unit digit of 7⁹⁵? 95 ÷ 4 leaves 3 → third in the cycle (7, 9, **3**, 1) → **3**.

## 2. Percentage, profit and loss
- x% of y = y% of x.
- If price rises by r%, reduce consumption by r/(100 + r) × 100% to keep spending the same.
- Profit % = profit/CP × 100. SP = CP × (100 + P%)/100.
- Successive changes a% and b%: net = a + b + ab/100.

**Example:** price up 20%, then down 20%: net = 20 − 20 − 400/100 = **−4%**.

## 3. Ratio, average, mixture
- Average = sum/number. If one value changes by d, average changes by d/n.
- **Alligation**: (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper) = cheaper : dearer quantities.

**Example:** average of 5 numbers is 20; one number 30 is replaced by 10. New average = 20 − 20/5 = **16**.

## 4. Time and work, time and distance
- If A does a job in a days and B in b days: together **ab/(a + b)** days.
- Speed = distance/time; km/h × 5/18 = m/s.
- Average speed for equal distances at x and y: **2xy/(x + y)**.
- Trains: crossing a pole: length/speed; crossing a platform: (train + platform)/speed; opposite directions: add speeds.

**Example:** A takes 10 days, B 15 days. Together: 150/25 = **6 days**.
**Example:** 54 km/h = 54 × 5/18 = **15 m/s**.

## 5. Simple and compound interest
- SI = PRT/100. CI: A = P(1 + R/100)ᵀ.
- For 2 years: **CI − SI = P(R/100)²**.

**Example:** P = ₹10,000, R = 10%, 2 years: CI − SI = 10,000 × 0.01 = **₹100**.

## 6. Reasoning
| Type | Tip |
|---|---|
| Series | check differences, then ratios, then squares/cubes, then alternate terms |
| Coding–decoding | write letter positions (A=1 … Z=26); reverse (A=26 … Z=1); EJOTY = 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 |
| Blood relations | draw a family tree with + (male) and − (female) |
| Direction | draw it; right of north is east; shadows in the morning fall to the west |
| Calendar | odd days: ordinary year 1, leap year 2; century leap only if divisible by 400 |
| Clock | angle = \|30H − 5.5M\|; hands coincide 11 times in 12 hours |
| Syllogism | use Venn diagrams; "some" includes "all" possibility |
| Seating | fix the most certain person first |

**Example (series):** 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ? → differences 4, 6, 8, 10, **12** → **42**.
**Example (clock):** angle at 3:40 = |90 − 220| = **130°**.
**Example (calendar):** 15 Aug 1947 was a **Friday**.

## 7. Data interpretation
- Read the units and the base year first. Approximate when options are far apart.
- Percentage share = part/total × 100. Growth = (new − old)/old × 100.

## 8. Comprehension (Hindi and English)
- Read the questions first, then the passage.
- Answer only from the passage; avoid outside knowledge and extreme words ("always", "never").

## 9. Elementary maths for RO/ARO (prelims GS)
Same topics at class-10 level: number system, percentage, average, ratio, SI/CI, time and work, simple geometry (area of circle πr², triangle ½bh, rectangle lb; Pythagoras).

## Exam traps
- Successive +20% and −20% is a 4% loss, not zero.
- Average speed over equal distances is the harmonic mean, not the simple average.
- CI − SI for 2 years = P(R/100)²; for 3 years = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100).
- 1900 was not a leap year; 2000 was.
