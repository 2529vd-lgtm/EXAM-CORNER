> **UPESSC Economics (Code 43): extra topics, unit by unit.** These are syllabus points that the ten unit notes cover only briefly. Read the unit notes first, then this page. Unit numbers here follow the **official UPESSC syllabus**.

## Unit 1: Micro-economic analysis (extra)
### Marshallian vs Hicksian demand
| Marshallian (ordinary) | Hicksian (compensated) |
|---|---|
| maximise utility given income | minimise expenditure given utility |
| x(p, M) | h(p, U) |
| includes income + substitution effect | substitution effect only |
| price change moves along it with real income changing | always slopes down |
- **Lancaster** (1966): goods are bundles of characteristics; consumers demand characteristics (explains new products, brand choice).

### Managerial theories of the firm
| Theory | Objective | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Baumol** (1959) | maximise **sales revenue** subject to a minimum profit | output higher and price lower than profit-maximiser; advertising always raises sales |
| **Marris** (1964) | maximise **balanced growth rate** of demand and capital, subject to security (valuation ratio) | managers trade profit for growth |
| **Williamson** (1964) | maximise **managerial utility** U(S, M, I_D): staff spending, managerial emoluments, discretionary investment | "expense preference" |
| Cyert–March | behavioural; satisficing, organisational slack | |

### Theory of limit price
- Firms set a price **below the profit-maximising level** to deter entry (Bain 1956; **Sylos-Labini's postulate**: entrants expect incumbents to keep output unchanged; Modigliani 1958 formalised it).
- Barriers: economies of scale, absolute cost advantage, product differentiation, capital requirements.
- **Contestable markets** (Baumol, Panzar, Willig, 1982): with free entry and exit, even a monopolist prices at average cost.

## Unit 2: Macro-economic analysis (extra)
### Post-Keynesian approach
- Kaldor, Joan Robinson, Kalecki, Pasinetti: distribution and growth linked; **Kaldor's saving function**: S = s_w·W + s_p·P with s_p > s_w; profit share rises with investment. Endogenous money (Kaldor, Moore).
- **Kalecki**: "workers spend what they get, capitalists get what they spend"; political business cycle (1943).

### Business cycle models
| Model | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Samuelson** (1939) | multiplier–accelerator interaction; path depends on values of MPC (c) and accelerator (v): damped, explosive or constant cycles |
| **Hicks** (1950) | multiplier–accelerator with a **ceiling** (full employment) and a **floor** (autonomous investment) → cycles are bounded |
| **Kaldor** (1940) | **non-linear** (S-shaped) saving and investment functions → endogenous limit cycles |
| **Hawtrey** | purely **monetary**: bank credit expansion and contraction cause cycles; "trade cycle is a purely monetary phenomenon" |
| Hayek | monetary over-investment |
| Schumpeter | innovation clusters |
| Real business cycle | technology shocks (Kydland–Prescott) |

### Fiscal and monetary policy in IS–LM
- Fiscal expansion: Y ↑, r ↑ (crowding out). Monetary: Y ↑, r ↓. **Policy mix**: fiscal + monetary together keeps r constant.
- Effectiveness depends on slopes: steep LM (low money demand elasticity) → monetary strong; flat LM → fiscal strong.

## Unit 3: Development and planning (extra)
### Measures
- **HDI** (UNDP, 1990): health, education, income; geometric mean. India's HDI rank **130** of 193 (HDR 2025, data for 2023).
- **GDI** (Gender Development Index) = female HDI / male HDI. **GII** (Gender Inequality Index): reproductive health, empowerment, labour market (higher = worse).

### Theories of development
| Theory | Key ideas |
|---|---|
| **Classical** (Smith, Ricardo, Malthus) | Smith: division of labour, market size, capital accumulation; Ricardo: diminishing returns in land → **stationary state**; Malthus: population checks |
| **Marx** | surplus value, capital accumulation, **reserve army of labour**, falling rate of profit, crises, stages (feudalism → capitalism → socialism) |
| **Schumpeter** (*Theory of Economic Development*, 1911) | **circular flow** disturbed by **innovation** by the **entrepreneur**, financed by bank credit; **creative destruction**; five types of innovation (new good, method, market, source of supply, organisation) |
| **Rostow** (1960) | five stages; take-off needs investment > 10% of NI |
| **Myrdal** (1957) | **circular cumulative causation**: **backwash effects** (rich regions pull labour and capital) stronger than **spread effects** → inequality widens |
| Nurkse | vicious circle of poverty (supply and demand side); balanced growth |
| Leibenstein | critical minimum effort |
| Rosenstein-Rodan | big push |
| Lewis | unlimited supply of labour |
| Hirschman | unbalanced growth, linkages |
| Nelson | low-level equilibrium trap |

### Investment criteria
| Criterion | Author | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital turnover** (lowest capital–output ratio) | Buchanan, Polak | choose projects with quick output per unit of capital |
| **Social marginal productivity (SMP)** | **Kahn (1951), Chenery (1953)** | maximise social (not private) returns, using shadow prices |
| **Marginal per capita reinvestment quotient** | **Galenson and Leibenstein** (1955) | choose capital-intensive techniques that generate surplus for reinvestment |
| **Time series criterion** | **Amartya Sen** | compare output streams over time |
| Capital intensity / choice of techniques | Dobb, Sen | labour-intensive (employment) vs capital-intensive (growth) |

- **Appropriate technology**: E.F. Schumacher, *Small is Beautiful* (1973), "intermediate technology".
- **Cost–benefit analysis**: NPV = Σ(Bₜ − Cₜ)/(1 + r)ᵗ; accept if NPV > 0; **IRR** (rate where NPV = 0) > cost of capital; benefit–cost ratio > 1. Social CBA uses **shadow prices** (Little–Mirrlees 1968, **UNIDO guidelines** 1972).

### Planning
- Techniques: input–output models, linear programming, consistency models, **Mahalanobis four-sector model**.
- **Planning in a market-oriented economy** = **indicative planning** (France; India after 1991): government sets targets and incentives, not commands.
- **NITI Aayog** (1 Jan 2015): think tank; cooperative and competitive federalism; **SDG India Index** (from 2018); Aspirational Districts (2018) and Blocks (2023) programmes.
- **SDGs**: 17 goals, 169 targets, Agenda 2030 (adopted Sept 2015).

## Unit 4: Growth economics (extra)
- Growth vs development vs **sustainable development** (Brundtland 1987).
- **Harrod**: G = s/v (actual), Gw = s/vᵣ (warranted, vᵣ = required capital–output ratio), Gn (natural). If G > Gw → cumulative expansion (knife-edge).
- **Domar**: investment has dual character: raises demand (multiplier) and capacity (σ = output–capital ratio); required growth of investment = s·σ.
- **Solow with technical progress**: Y = F(K, AL); steady state growth of per-capita output = growth of A (g). Growth accounting: g_Y = α·g_K + (1 − α)·g_L + TFP growth.

## Unit 5: Money and banking (extra)
### Organisation of India's money market
| Organised | Unorganised |
|---|---|
| RBI, commercial banks, cooperative banks, DFIs, NBFCs, primary dealers | moneylenders, indigenous bankers (shroffs, chettiars), chit funds |
- Instruments: call money, notice money (2–14 days), term money, T-bills (91, 182, 364 days), CPs, CDs, repo, **TREPS** (tri-party repo).

### Changing role of RBI
1935–1969 regulator; 1969–1991 credit planning and directed lending; after 1991 deregulation, prudential norms, **inflation targeting (2016)**, digital payments regulator (UPI, CBDC), consumer protection (Integrated Ombudsman 2021).

### Inflation in India
- Measures: CPI (combined; base 2012), WPI (base 2011-12), GDP deflator.
- Trends: high (about 9–10%) in 2010–13 → moderation after inflation targeting → low (about 1.7% average in Apr–Dec 2025) → rising again in 2026 (repo raised to 5.50%).
- Consequences: redistribution from creditors to debtors, fall in real wages, uncertainty. Remedies: monetary (repo, CRR), fiscal, supply-side.

### Financial sector reforms
Narasimham I (1991) and II (1998), prudential norms, SARFAESI 2002, payments and small finance banks (2015), **IBC 2016**, PSB mergers (2019–20), **NARCL** (2021), digital lending guidelines (2022).

### Functions of commercial banks
Primary: accepting deposits, lending (loans, overdrafts, cash credit, discounting bills). Secondary: agency (collecting cheques, paying bills) and utility (lockers, letters of credit, remittances). **Credit creation**.

## Unit 6: Public finance (extra)
### Budget
- **Components**: revenue budget (revenue receipts: tax + non-tax; revenue expenditure) and capital budget (capital receipts: borrowings, recoveries, disinvestment; capital expenditure).
- **Kinds**: balanced, surplus, deficit; **performance budget** (USA, Hoover Commission); **outcome budget** (India 2005); **zero-base budgeting** (Peter Pyhrr, Texas Instruments, 1970; every item justified afresh); **gender budgeting** (India from 2005-06; Gender Budget Statement in the Union Budget); green budget.
- Plan/non-plan distinction removed in 2017-18; Railway budget merged in 2017; budget date moved to 1 Feb.
- **Formulation and enactment**: preparation (Department of Economic Affairs) → presentation (Art. 112) → general discussion → departmental standing committees → **voting on demands for grants (Art. 113)** → **Appropriation Bill (Art. 114)** → Finance Bill → **vote on account (Art. 116)** if needed; guillotine for remaining demands.
- **Types of deficit** and effects: see Unit 6 notes (revenue, fiscal, primary, effective revenue, monetised).

### Public revenue
- Approaches to dividing the tax burden: **cost of service**, **benefit** (Lindahl, Wicksell), **ability to pay** (objective: income/wealth; subjective: sacrifice).
- **Tax elasticity** = % change in tax revenue / % change in GDP, **excluding** discretionary changes. **Tax buoyancy** = the same **including** discretionary changes (new taxes, rate changes). Buoyancy > 1: revenue grows faster than GDP.
- **Taxable capacity** (Dalton): **absolute** (maximum tax without harmful effects) and **relative** (comparison between regions/countries); depends on income, its distribution, tax structure, purpose of spending.

### Public debt
- Objectives: finance deficits, development, war, stabilisation. Sources: internal (market loans, small savings, T-bills) and external.
- Burden: direct money burden (external debt), real burden (distribution); **management**: refunding, conversion, sinking fund, debt-to-GDP anchor (FRBM; Union target about 50% by 2030).

### Fiscal federalism problems in India
Vertical imbalance; dependence of states on transfers; centrally sponsored schemes with conditions; cesses and surcharges outside the divisible pool; GST compensation ended (2022); borrowing limits on states (Art. 293).

### Fiscal policy
- Objectives: growth, stability, equity, employment. Instruments: taxation, public spending, public debt, deficit financing.
- **Compensatory fiscal policy** (Hansen, Keynes): spend more in depression, less in boom (built-in stabilisers + discretionary). **Pump priming**: one-time spending to restart private demand.
- **Functional finance** (**Abba Lerner**, 1943): judge fiscal policy by its effects on the economy (full employment, price stability), not by whether the budget balances.
- In developing economies: capital formation, resource mobilisation, reducing inequality, price stability.

## Unit 7: International economics (extra)
- **Haberler** (1936): **opportunity cost** version of comparative advantage using the production possibility curve; removes the labour theory of value. Constant cost → straight PPF (complete specialisation); increasing cost → concave PPF (incomplete specialisation).
- **Gains from trade**: consumption beyond PPF; static (specialisation) and dynamic (technology, scale) gains.
- **Factors influencing terms of trade**: reciprocal demand, elasticities, tariffs, devaluation, technical progress, economic growth (export-biased vs import-biased).
- **Problem of international liquidity**: shortage of acceptable reserves to finance trade; **Triffin dilemma**; **SDRs** created by the IMF in **1969**. SDR basket: **US dollar, euro, Chinese renminbi, Japanese yen, British pound** (renminbi added 2016).
- **Tariff and non-tariff barriers**: tariffs (ad valorem, specific, compound); non-tariff: quotas, **voluntary export restraints**, licensing, SPS and TBT standards, anti-dumping duties, subsidies. **Partial equilibrium** (one market: consumer/producer surplus) vs **general equilibrium** (PPF and offer curves; terms of trade effects) analysis of a tariff.
- **Multilateral bodies**: **IMF** (BoP support, surveillance, SDRs; quota-based voting); **World Bank Group**: IBRD (1944), **IDA** (1960, soft loans), **IFC** (private sector), **MIGA**, **ICSID**; **WTO** (1995; dispute settlement; MFN, national treatment; TRIPS, TRIMS, GATS, AoA).

## Unit 8: Indian Economy I (extra)
- **Characteristics of a developing economy**: low per-capita income, dependence on agriculture, high population growth, unemployment and underemployment, low capital formation, dualism, inequality.
- **Energy**: India met its target of **50% installed power capacity from non-fossil sources in 2025**, five years ahead of the 2030 NDC goal; coal still provides most electricity generation. Renewable: solar (largest addition), wind, hydro, biomass, nuclear (non-fossil but not renewable).
- **Agriculture**: institutional aspects (land tenure, credit, markets, cooperatives) and technological aspects (HYV, irrigation, fertiliser, mechanisation). **National Agricultural Policy 2000**.
- **Land reforms**: zamindari abolition, tenancy reforms, ceilings, consolidation; digitisation (DILRMP, SVAMITVA 2020).
- **Rural credit**: NABARD (1982), RRBs (1975), cooperatives (PACS), **Kisan Credit Card (1998)**, **priority sector lending 40%** of ANBC (18% agriculture), SHG–bank linkage.
- **Agricultural price policy**: CACP (1965), MSP, procurement, buffer stock, **FCI (1965)**; MSP at least 1.5× A2 + FL cost since 2018-19.
- **PDS and food security**: universal PDS → **TPDS (1997)** → **NFSA 2013** (75% rural, 50% urban; 5 kg/person; Antyodaya 35 kg/household) → **One Nation One Ration Card** → free grains under PMGKAY extended to 2028.
- **India's population**: 121.09 crore (2011); decadal growth 17.7%; sex ratio 943; TFR 2.0 (NFHS-5); rising working-age share (**demographic dividend** till about 2040s); Census 2027.

## Unit 9: Indian Economy II (extra)
- **Industrial policy reforms**: 1991 (delicensing, MRTP limits removed, FDI liberalised, PSU reservation cut), Make in India (2014), **PLI** (2020).
- **MSME**: classification revised in Budget 2025-26: **micro** investment up to ₹2.5 crore and turnover up to ₹10 crore; **small** ₹25 crore / ₹100 crore; **medium** ₹125 crore / ₹500 crore. Udyam registration (2020). MSMEs give about 30% of GDP and about 45% of exports.
- **Foreign trade**: composition (exports: petroleum products, engineering goods, electronics, pharma, gems; imports: crude oil, electronics, gold, coal); direction (USA largest export market; China and UAE large import sources). Persistent merchandise trade deficit; services surplus (IT); remittances largest in the world.
- **Trade reforms**: tariff cuts after 1991, FTP 2023 (no end date), rupee trade settlement, FTAs (UAE 2022, Australia 2022, EFTA 2024/25, UK 2025, EU concluded 2026).
- **Exchange rate reforms**: devaluation 1991 → LERMS 1992 → unified market rate 1993 → current account convertibility 1994; managed float.
- **India and WTO**: food security (public stockholding, peace clause, Bali 2013), agricultural subsidies, e-commerce moratorium, fisheries subsidies.
- **FDI**: automatic and government routes; caps (defence 74% automatic, more via government; insurance raised to **100%** in Budget 2025-26 for companies investing all premium in India); top sources: Mauritius, Singapore, USA.
- **Poverty and inequality estimates**: Tendulkar 21.9% (2011-12), Rangarajan 29.5%; **NITI MPI**: 29.17% (2013-14) → 11.28% (2022-23); consumption **Gini about 25.5** (World Bank, 2022-23 data: among the more equal countries by consumption); income/wealth inequality much higher (World Inequality Lab).
- **Unemployment**: PLFS usual status about 3.2% (2023-24); youth and educated unemployment higher; female LFPR rising.
- **HDI**: rank 130 (HDR 2025).

## Unit 10: Statistical methods (extra)
- **Sampling vs census**: census = every unit (accurate, costly); sampling = part (cheaper, faster).
- **Errors**: sampling errors (fall as n rises) and **non-sampling errors** (bias, non-response, measurement, processing; can rise with large surveys).
- **Sampling of attributes**: test of proportion: z = (p − P)/√(PQ/n); standard error of proportion √(PQ/n).
- Sampling distributions: t (small samples, unknown σ), χ² (variance, goodness of fit, independence), F (ratio of variances, ANOVA).
- Time series analysis: components (trend, seasonal, cyclical, irregular); additive (Y = T + S + C + I) and multiplicative (Y = T × S × C × I) models; moving averages; least squares trend.
