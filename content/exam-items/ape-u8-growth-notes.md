## 1. Growth vs development
- **Growth** = rise in real output/per-capita income. **Development** = growth + structural change + better well-being (health, education, freedom).
- **Amartya Sen**: development as freedom; **capabilities and functionings**; *Development as Freedom* (1999); Nobel 1998.
- **Human Development Index** (UNDP, from 1990; Mahbub ul Haq with Sen): health (life expectancy), education (mean and expected years of schooling), standard of living (GNI per capita, PPP $). Geometric mean of the three indices since 2010.
- Related: IHDI (inequality-adjusted), GDI, GII, **MPI** (Alkire–Foster, 2010).

## 2. Growth models

### Harrod–Domar (1939, 1946)
- **g = s / v** (s = saving rate, v = capital–output ratio).
- Three growth rates: actual (g), **warranted** (gw = s/v), **natural** (gn = labour force growth + technical progress).
- **Knife-edge instability**: any gap between g and gw widens.
- Long-run full employment needs g = gw = gn: unlikely ("golden age", Joan Robinson).
- Basis of India's early Five-Year Plans.

### Solow–Swan neoclassical model (1956)
- Y = F(K, AL) with diminishing returns and substitution between K and L.
- Capital accumulation: **Δk = s·f(k) − (n + δ)k**.
- **Steady state** where s·f(k*) = (n + δ)k*: output per worker stops growing without technical progress.
- Long-run growth of per-capita income = rate of **technical progress** (exogenous).
- **Golden rule** (Phelps, 1961): consumption per worker is maximum where f′(k) = n + δ (MPK = n + δ).
- **Conditional convergence**: poorer economies grow faster if they share the same steady state.
- **Solow residual** = growth not explained by K and L = total factor productivity (TFP).

## 4. Poverty and inequality
### Measures of inequality
- **Lorenz curve** and **Gini coefficient** = A/(A + B), from 0 (equality) to 1.
- **Palma ratio**: share of top 10% / share of bottom 40%.
- **Theil index**, Atkinson index (with inequality aversion parameter).

![Lorenz curve](uploads/diagrams/lorenz.svg)

### Poverty measures
- **Head-count ratio** (H): share of people below the poverty line.
- **Poverty gap index**: average shortfall from the line.
- **Squared poverty gap** (severity). **FGT family** (Foster–Greer–Thorbecke): P(α); α = 0 head count, 1 gap, 2 severity.
- **Sen index**: combines H, income gap and Gini among the poor.
- **Global MPI** (UNDP + OPHI): 10 indicators in health, education, living standards.

## 5. Development and trade/finance
- **Two-gap model** (Chenery & Strout, 1966): saving gap and foreign exchange gap; foreign aid fills the larger gap.
- Import substitution vs export-led growth (East Asia).
- **Washington Consensus** (John Williamson, 1989).

## Exam traps
- Harrod–Domar: g = s/v (not s × v).
- In the Solow model, higher saving raises the **level** of per-capita income, not its long-run **growth rate**.
- Hirschman favoured **unbalanced** growth; Nurkse favoured **balanced** growth.
- Rostow's take-off needs investment above 10% of national income.
- Kuznets curve is about inequality; the environmental Kuznets curve (Unit 9) is about pollution.
