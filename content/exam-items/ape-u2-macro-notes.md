> **Unit 2 · Macro Economics.** Usually 10–15% of the paper. Focus on national income identities, multipliers, IS–LM, consumption theories, inflation and the schools of thought.

## 1. National income accounting
- **GDP** = value of final goods and services produced *within* the country in a year.
- **GNP = GDP + NFIA** (net factor income from abroad).
- **NDP = GDP − depreciation**. **NNP at factor cost = National Income** = NNP at market price − net indirect taxes (indirect taxes − subsidies).
- Three methods: **product (value added)**, **income** (wages + rent + interest + profit + mixed income), **expenditure**: Y = C + I + G + (X − M).
- **GDP deflator** = Nominal GDP / Real GDP × 100.
- **Green GDP** adjusts for resource depletion. Transfer payments and second-hand sales are **excluded**.
- India: base year **2011-12** (CSO/NSO), GVA at basic prices + product taxes − product subsidies = GDP. (A new GDP series with a more recent base year has been announced; check MoSPI.)

## 2. Classical vs Keynesian

| Point | Classical | Keynes (*General Theory*, 1936) |
|---|---|---|
| Employment | full employment is normal | under-employment equilibrium possible |
| Say's law | supply creates its own demand | rejected; effective demand decides output |
| Money | neutral, quantity theory | affects interest and output |
| Wages/prices | flexible | sticky downward |
| Saving = investment via | interest rate | income changes |
| Policy | laissez-faire | active fiscal policy |

- **Effective demand** is the point where aggregate demand = aggregate supply.
- **Paradox of thrift**: if everyone saves more, total saving may not rise because income falls.

## 3. Multiplier and accelerator

![Keynesian cross](uploads/diagrams/keynesian-cross.svg)

| Multiplier | Formula |
|---|---|
| Investment multiplier (closed, no tax) | k = 1 / (1 − MPC) = 1 / MPS |
| With proportional tax t | 1 / [1 − c(1 − t)] |
| Open economy | 1 / (MPS + MPM) |
| Tax multiplier | −c / (1 − c) |
| **Balanced budget multiplier** | **= 1** (Haavelmo) |

- Multiplier concept: **R.F. Kahn** (1931, employment multiplier); Keynes used the investment multiplier.
- **Accelerator** (J.M. Clark): investment depends on the *change* in output: I = v·ΔY.
- **Samuelson's multiplier–accelerator interaction** (1939) explains business cycles; **Hicks** added ceilings and floors.

## 4. Consumption theories

| Theory | Author | Main idea |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute income | Keynes | C = a + bY; APC falls as income rises; MPC < APC |
| Relative income | **Duesenberry** (1949) | demonstration effect; **ratchet effect** (consumption does not fall easily) |
| Permanent income | **Friedman** (1957) | C depends on permanent (expected long-run) income; transitory income is saved |
| Life cycle | **Modigliani**, Ando, Brumberg | people smooth consumption over life; save in working years |
| Random walk | **Hall** (1978) | with rational expectations, consumption follows a random walk |

- **Kuznets' puzzle**: long-run US data showed constant APC (≈ 0.9), short-run data showed falling APC. Friedman and Modigliani resolved it.

## 5. Investment
- **Marginal efficiency of capital (MEC)**: discount rate that makes PV of returns = cost (Keynes). Invest while MEC > r.
- **MEI** (Lerner) accounts for rising cost of capital goods.
- **Tobin's q** = market value of firm / replacement cost of capital. q > 1 → invest.
- Neoclassical (Jorgenson) user cost of capital.

## 6. IS–LM (Hicks 1937, Hansen)
- **IS**: goods market equilibrium (I = S); slopes **down**.
- **LM**: money market equilibrium (L = M/P); slopes **up**.
- Fiscal expansion → IS shifts right → Y ↑ and r ↑ (partial **crowding out**).
- Monetary expansion → LM shifts right → Y ↑ and r ↓.

![IS–LM](uploads/diagrams/is-lm.svg)

| Case | Shape | Effective policy |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquidity trap** (Keynesian range) | LM horizontal | fiscal fully effective, monetary useless |
| Classical range | LM vertical | monetary fully effective, fiscal useless (full crowding out) |
| Investment interest-inelastic | IS vertical | fiscal only |

- **Pigou (real balance) effect**: falling prices raise real wealth, so consumption rises even in a liquidity trap.
- **IS–LM–BP / Mundell–Fleming**: under perfect capital mobility, **fixed exchange rate → fiscal policy effective**; **floating exchange rate → monetary policy effective**.

## 7. Aggregate demand and supply
- AD slopes down (Pigou wealth effect, Keynes interest-rate effect, Mundell–Fleming exchange-rate effect).
- SRAS slopes up (sticky wages/prices); **LRAS vertical** at potential output.
- Supply shock → **stagflation** (output ↓, prices ↑).

## 8. Inflation and unemployment
- Types: **demand-pull**, **cost-push**, structural (Latin American structuralists), creeping, galloping, hyperinflation.
- **Inflationary gap** (Keynes): excess of AD over full-employment output.
- **Phillips curve** (A.W. Phillips, 1958): inverse relation between wage inflation and unemployment (UK data 1861–1957). Samuelson & Solow applied it to price inflation.
- **Friedman–Phelps** (1967–68): expectations-augmented Phillips curve; vertical in the long run at the **natural rate of unemployment** (NAIRU).
- **Rational expectations** (Muth 1961, Lucas, Sargent, Wallace): anticipated policy is ineffective (**policy ineffectiveness proposition**); **Lucas critique**.
- **Okun's law**: 1% unemployment above natural rate ≈ 2–3% output gap.
- Sacrifice ratio: % output lost per 1% cut in inflation.
- Unemployment types: frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal, disguised (Joan Robinson; Nurkse in LDCs).

## 9. Business cycles
| Theory | Author |
|---|---|
| Multiplier–accelerator | Samuelson, Hicks |
| Innovation theory | Schumpeter |
| Monetary over-investment | Hayek |
| Purely monetary | Hawtrey |
| Real business cycle | Kydland & Prescott (technology shocks) |
| Political business cycle | Nordhaus |
| Cobweb (agriculture, not macro) | Kaldor / Ezekiel |
- Cycle lengths: **Kitchin** ≈ 3–4 yrs (inventory), **Juglar** ≈ 7–11 yrs (investment), **Kuznets** ≈ 15–25 yrs (building), **Kondratieff** ≈ 45–60 yrs (long waves).

## 10. Schools of thought (quick map)
| School | Key names | Core claim |
|---|---|---|
| Classical | Smith, Ricardo, Say, Mill | markets self-correct |
| Keynesian | Keynes, Hansen, Hicks | demand management |
| Monetarist | Friedman | "inflation is always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon"; k% rule |
| New Classical | Lucas, Sargent, Barro | rational expectations; **Ricardian equivalence** (Barro) |
| New Keynesian | Mankiw, Akerlof, Stiglitz | menu costs, efficiency wages, sticky prices with RE |
| Supply-side | Laffer | tax cuts raise output |
| Post-Keynesian | Kaldor, Joan Robinson, Kalecki | distribution and growth |

## Exam traps
- Balanced budget multiplier = 1, not 0.
- In a liquidity trap, the LM curve is horizontal and **monetary** policy fails.
- MPC + MPS = 1; APC + APS = 1.
- Under fixed exchange rates with perfect capital mobility, monetary policy is ineffective.
- Permanent income hypothesis: a one-time bonus is mostly saved.
